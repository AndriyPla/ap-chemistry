# AP Chemistry Unit 2: Compound Structure and Properties

## 200 Original AP-Style Practice Questions

This bank contains original questions aligned to the seven topics in Unit 2 of the AP Chemistry Course and Exam Description (CED). Public Quizlet sets were used only to identify common vocabulary, emphases, and misconceptions. No question was copied from Quizlet, AP Classroom, a secure practice exam, or a released AP question.

### Suggested use

- Questions marked **MCQ** are selected-response items.
- Questions marked **CR** are short constructed-response items. A strong response should make a claim and connect it to chemical evidence.
- Unless stated otherwise, assume substances are at standard laboratory conditions.
- Answer keys give the expected idea; equivalent scientifically correct reasoning is acceptable.
- Questions grouped under a stimulus should be answered from the supplied graph, model, diagram, or data table as well as prior chemical knowledge.

### Answers 1–25

1 B; 2 B; 3 C; 4 B; 5 A; 6 B; 7 A; 8 C; 9 B; 10 C; 11 B; 12 C; 13 A; 14 B; 15 D.  
16. Identical atoms have equal electronegativity, so the pair is shared equally.  
17. Polar covalent; N attracts shared electrons more strongly than H.  
18. Bonding is a continuum of electron sharing/transfer; many bonds have partial ionic character.  
19. Ionic attraction between Ca²⁺ and CO₃²⁻; covalent C–O bonds within carbonate.  
20. P–Cl, because the P/Cl electronegativity difference is larger.  
21. X is likely a metal that forms X²⁺; elemental conductivity is consistent with mobile electrons.  
22. Cl₂ is symmetric; HCl has greater electron density near Cl.  
23. Polar bond dipoles can cancel because of molecular geometry.  
24. Covalent molecular bonding; shared electron pairs produce neutral SiCl₄ molecules.  
25. Covalent electrons are localized between particular atoms; metallic valence electrons are delocalized across many atoms.

41. Equilibrium distance is the x-coordinate of B’s minimum (about 120 pm); bond-energy magnitude is the vertical energy difference from the minimum to zero (about 600 kJ mol⁻¹).

43. N₂ has the deeper well; its equilibrium distance is also shorter in standard comparisons.  
44. It decreases.  
45. Nucleus-nucleus and electron-electron repulsions rise sharply.  
46. Bond breaking absorbs energy; bond formation releases energy.  
47. The processes are reverses along the same energy path.  
48. Equal dissociation energies; different bond lengths. No necessary conclusion about atom identity from those facts alone.  
49. Repulsive, pushing the atoms apart.  
50. The N≡N triple bond has greater bond energy and requires more energy to disrupt.
